Overview

Bridging the Gap
in Real-World 3D Scene Datasets

Existing 3D scene datasets face significant limitations. Real-world datasets reconstructed from RGB-D scans often suffer from occlusions and incomplete spatial coverage, while simulated datasets exhibit a domain gap from real-world environments and are typically limited in scene complexity, with insufficient diversity in scene categories.

To address these limitations, we introduce NaviGS, a real-world dataset comprising high-fidelity 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations and meshes. Based on the reconstructed 3DGS, we render high-quality RGB images and depth observations from different viewpoints. Furthermore, NaviGS facilitates research on spatial intelligence and supports a range of downstream tasks, including 3D scene understanding, controllable scene generation, and embodied agent navigation.

NaviGS contains 120 high-fidelity scenes covering more than 50,000 m², spanning more than 10 scene categories, including meeting rooms, offices, classrooms, gyms, residential environments, corridors, exhibition halls, and outdoor gardens. In its initial release, we will provide five RGB scenes and five semantic scenes.

Real-World Capture

All scenes captured in real environments, eliminating the sim-to-real domain gap that plagues synthetic datasets.

Hierarchical Scene Graphs

For a subset of scenes, we release hierarchical 3D scene graphs following the floor–room–stuff–object structure.

First Release

Applications

Powering Spatial Intelligence Research

NaviGS supports a broad range of downstream tasks in 3D vision and embodied AI.

3D Scene Understanding

Multi-modal annotations enable research in scene parsing, object detection, semantic segmentation, and structural reasoning.

Controllable Scene Generation

3DGS representations and scene graphs provide a foundation for text-driven scene editing, layout generation, and novel view synthesis.

Embodied Agent Navigation

Real-world multi-room scenes with hierarchical graphs support long-horizon navigation, VLA models, and spatial planning research.
